Hindsight
A macro time machine. $10,000, a hidden five-year window between 1925 and today, three decisions.

Which year is it?

You wake up somewhere between 1925 and 2021 with $10,000 and a newspaper. Read the wire, read the macro tape, place your chips across stocks, bonds, gold, oil, currencies, housing, crypto and cash. Then live with it for five years.

  1. Spread ten $1,000 chips across up to seven options. Max four chips on any one.
  2. Before the clock moves, call which option will win the next stretch.
  3. At year 2 and year 4, hold or rebalance with fresh news.
  4. After year five, the board flips and shows the year.

Each puzzle draws options that actually traded in that window: sterling in the 1930s, the yen once currencies float in the 1970s, the Nasdaq-100, single stocks, silver and bitcoin as they arrive. Cash earns the T-bill rate. Rebalancing re-deals all ten chips at a tenth of your new total each.

Data and methods
  • S&P 500, 10-year Treasuries, 3-month T-bills, Baa corporate bonds, US housing and gold, 1928–2025: Aswath Damodaran, NYU Stern (annual total returns).
  • 1924–1927 stocks and bonds: computed from Robert Shiller's monthly data (December averages plus dividends; bond returns from long yields). T-bills 1926–27 from Ibbotson; 1924–25 estimated. Gold fixed at $20.67 until 1934.
  • Inflation: BLS CPI-U, December to December. 10-year yield: Shiller to 1952, Federal Reserve after. CAPE: Shiller, through 2022.
  • Crude oil: EIA U.S. first purchase price, annual average. Natural gas: Henry Hub December average.
  • Pound: annual averages from historical parity and market records before 1971, Federal Reserve G.5A after. Yen, Swiss franc, euro: Federal Reserve annual averages. Currency returns are spot moves only, no interest.
  • Nasdaq-100, Apple, Nvidia, silver, REITs, long Treasuries, US bond index, bitcoin: calendar-year returns from ChartRow (dividends reinvested where paid).
  • The results chart is monthly. Stocks, Treasuries, gold, oil, gas, yen, franc, euro, silver and bitcoin follow their real monthly moves; corporate and index bonds follow Treasury moves, and the Nasdaq-100, single stocks and REITs follow the S&P 500’s monthly pattern. Each year is adjusted to land exactly on its annual return.
  • Mixed conventions (year-end vs. annual average) mean a few series are smoother than a trader would have felt. It is a game about the past, not advice.
